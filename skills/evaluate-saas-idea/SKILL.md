# Evaluate a SaaS Idea

Assess the idea as a real investment decision. Be direct, evidence-led, and
commercially realistic. Do not soften material flaws, but do not perform
skepticism for effect: explain why each conclusion follows.

## Inputs

Use information already available in the conversation. The useful inputs are:

- SaaS idea
- Target audience
- Intended pricing
- Founder's technical skills, domain access, and unfair advantages
- Two or three comparable companies
- What customers do today instead

Ask only for missing inputs that would materially change the verdict. If the
user wants an immediate evaluation, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions.

## Evidence standard

- Separate verified facts, founder claims, assumptions, and inferences.
- Research current competitors, pricing, customer complaints, communities, and
  demand signals when current information would materially affect the verdict.
- Cite sources used. Never invent market size, search volume, revenue, or
  competitor traction.
- Treat stated interest as weak evidence. Prefer purchases, paid pilots,
  switching behavior, repeated urgent complaints, and costly workarounds.
- Express revenue scenarios as transparent assumptions and simple math, not a
  false-precision forecast.
- Surface a decisive dealbreaker early instead of burying it in the conclusion.

## Evaluation

Use these sections in order.

### 1. Problem Severity

Identify the pain, whether it is a must-have or nice-to-have, its frequency,
the cost of the current alternative, and what customers do when it occurs.

### 2. Customer Analysis

Name the most likely first paying customer, the highest-benefit segment, who
will never pay, and what a buyer must believe to purchase on day one. Prefer a
narrow beachhead over a broad persona.

### 3. Market Validation

State what would constitute strong demand evidence. Give concrete keywords,
communities, forums, subreddits, and customer conversations to investigate.
List signals that would falsify or weaken demand.

### 4. Competition Analysis

List direct competitors, indirect competitors, and the status quo. Explain why
customers would switch, why they would stay, and switching costs across time,
money, habit, workflow disruption, and data lock-in.

### 5. Monetization Potential

Judge willingness to pay and suitable pricing models. Estimate low, medium,
and high annual revenue scenarios with explicit customer-count and price
assumptions. Compare the opportunity with a median bootstrapped SaaS; a score
of 5 means average, not good.

### 6. Founder-Market Fit

Assess solo-founder suitability, build and maintenance load, customer access,
trust requirements, and hidden operational, regulatory, support, integration,
or data complexity.

### 7. Distribution

Give credible, stage-specific paths to the first 10, 100, and 1,000 paying
customers. Reject channels that do not match buyer behavior or founder access.

### 8. Risks

Rank the largest failure modes. Cover technical, market, and customer
acquisition risks. Distinguish existential risks from manageable execution
risks.

### 9. MVP Recommendation

Define the smallest version one developer can build in two weeks. Remove
nonessential features. State which assumptions must be validated before coding
and propose the cheapest credible validation test for each.

### 10. Final Verdict

Score relative to a median bootstrapped SaaS and include the direction of each
scale:

- Demand Score (1 = no meaningful demand, 10 = exceptional demand)
- Competition Score (1 = structurally weak position, 10 = highly defensible position)
- Monetization Score (1 = poor willingness to pay, 10 = exceptional economics)
- Distribution Difficulty (1 = easy, 10 = exceptionally hard)
- Solo Founder Suitability (1 = poor fit, 10 = excellent fit)

Then answer plainly:

- What is the single biggest dealbreaker, if any?
- Would you build this?
- Would you invest your own money?
- What would make the idea significantly stronger?

End with one verdict: `Pursue`, `Validate before building`, `Pivot`, or `Pass`.
Do not recommend building when a cheaper pre-build test can resolve the central
uncertainty.
